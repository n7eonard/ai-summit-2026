## Why this file exists

The talk argues the bottleneck moved, from one person's mission. This material suggests the
same movement is visible in how AI-native companies actually hire — which would turn a
first-person claim into an observable market shift.

That's a meaningful upgrade to Act 1, and it also answers [Q17](../meta/open-questions.md)
(the talk is ~60% first-person). But only if it's sourced.

## The signals

**The role absorbing product work has a name: Forward Deployed Engineer.** Unlike traditional
engineers, FDEs are embedded with a single customer and tasked with improving business
processes with technology. Reported: OpenAI FDEs turn research breakthroughs into production
systems; Anthropic's drive frontier-AI adoption by building bespoke LLM solutions for large
enterprises. a16z frames the wider pattern as *services-led growth* — startups deploying
technical teams directly into customers during early adoption instead of relying on
product-led growth.

**"T-shaped builders" over classical PMs.** AI-native companies reported as hiring fewer PMs,
or delaying their first PM hire as long as possible — wanting deep expertise in one domain
plus enough breadth to contribute elsewhere.

**The layer that's collapsing is the middle.** Attributed to Adam Judelson: the non-technical
PM is dead — the intermediate PM layers focused purely on stakeholder management and process
orchestration are the ones being automated.

**Product-mindedness as a baseline, not a specialisation.** Attributed to Gergely Orosz
(*The Pragmatic Engineer*): being more product-minded is becoming a baseline expectation at
startups.

**The counter-evidence, which matters more than the rest.** Mistral is reported to be actively
hiring PMs, with job descriptions explicitly open to strong engineering backgrounds moving into
product leadership. And Anthropic recruits PMs at very high compensation — but for a profile
radically unlike the classical PM.

**So the honest reading is not "the PM is disappearing."** It is that the coordination and
translation layers collapse, and what remains is sharp product thinking exercised by builders —
whatever the title. That *is* the compression thesis, arriving from the labour market instead
of from a mission. Note that it also contradicts the loose version of the argument, which is
why it's worth including rather than only the supportive half.

## The strongest framing found, and it's free

The **"chimera role"** — an engineer asked to carry product responsibility ends up doing
neither job properly, and the reported consequences are friction and burnout.

This is the sharpest available phrasing of [Shift 1](../docs/shifts/01-split-the-role.md), it
is aimed squarely at engineers, and the talk currently has **no concrete moment for engineers
at all** ([Q16](../meta/open-questions.md)). One slide would close both gaps at once.

The message isn't "learn product thinking." It's **"stop burning your engineers in a chimera
role."**
