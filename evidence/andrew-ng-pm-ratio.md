# Andrew Ng — the PM-to-engineer ratio

**Supports:** C5, C6 · **Slide:** 17 · **Verified:** 2026-08-24

## The claims

**C5 (quoted on the slide):** *"I don't see product management work becoming faster at the
same speed as engineering."*

**C6:** One of Ng's teams asked for a ratio of **two PMs per engineer**.

## Status — read before the slide ships

**C6 is verified.** The reported detail: a team proposed moving from 1 PM : 4 engineers to
**1 PM : 0.5 engineers** — i.e. two PMs per engineer — and Ng noted this "would have sounded
absurd a year ago."

**C5 needs one more pass.** The substance is consistently attested across secondary coverage
of Ng's remarks — that AI drastically accelerates coding while product decision-making does
not keep pace, and that the bottleneck has moved from *can we build this* to *should we build
this*. But the sources found so far are commentary **about** what he said, not the original.

A **direct quotation on a slide, attributed by name**, needs the primary: the specific issue
of *The Batch*, or the talk or interview it came from. Until then, either find it or
paraphrase and attribute the substance rather than the wording.

→ tracked in [`meta/open-questions.md`](../meta/open-questions.md)

## Why it's in the talk

It's independent corroboration arriving from the opposite direction. The talk reaches "the
bottleneck moved" from one freelancer's failed mission; Ng reaches it from running multiple
teams at scale. Two different vantage points, same conclusion, neither derived from the other.

Placement matters: it comes **after** the Bricks story, not before. First the audience watches
it happen, then someone credible says it's general. Reversed, it's an argument from authority.
