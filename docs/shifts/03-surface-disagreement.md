# Shift 3 — Make the disagreement visible before you make the thing

**Slides:** 24–25 · **Evidence:** field + [thesis §4](../thesis.md)

## The failure

You built the thing, and the thing became the first place where the disagreement was
finally visible to everyone. That's a very expensive way to run a meeting.

**From the field:** six people disagreed on what a good deal was. They had disagreed the
whole time. It cost six weeks of building to surface something that one uncomfortable
conversation would have surfaced on day one — and the reason the conversation didn't happen
is that nothing forced it, while the building was right there, available, and satisfying.

The single-expert version of the same thing, and the hardest sentence in the talk:

> **You cannot automate a decision your expert can't state twice the same way.**

Not because the expert is unreliable. Because what they're doing is genuinely richer than
the rule they'll give you when asked, and asking is the wrong instrument. You have to watch
them decide across cases, and put the variance in front of them.

## Why now specifically

When building was expensive, a spec review was cheap by comparison and so it happened. Now
building is cheaper than the meeting, and "let's just prototype it and see" has quietly
become the more expensive option — while still *feeling* like the pragmatic one.

That feeling is the trap. It was correct advice for twenty years.

## What to do

- **Get the variance in front of the expert.** Not "what's your rule" — they'll answer, and
  the answer will be sincere and incomplete. Instead: five past cases where they decided
  differently under the same stated rule, side by side. The disagreement becomes undeniable
  and, crucially, *theirs to resolve* rather than yours to guess at.
- **Force the disagreement into an artefact.** A written definition of "good" that six people
  have actually initialled. If nobody will sign it, you have your finding, and you got it in
  a day.
- **Treat "everyone agrees" as unverified until tested.** Nobody in the story thought
  they disagreed. Agreement that has never been stressed is indistinguishable from agreement
  that doesn't exist.
- **Bring people in at genesis, or take it to autonomous impact:**

> **The middle is where work dies.**

Either they're in from the beginning and it's theirs, or you carry it all the way to
demonstrated impact and bring them a result. The middle — a half-built thing shown to people
who weren't there at the start — collects opinions without collecting ownership, and every
opinion arrives too late to be cheap and too early to be evidence.

## Objections

**"Meetings don't create alignment either."** Correct, and the talk agrees — see
[`llm-as-mirror.md`](../llm-as-mirror.md). The goal is not agreement, it's *visible*
disagreement. Those are different deliverables and only the second one is reliably achievable.

**"This slows us down."** It slows down the building. It speeds up the being-right, which is
now the expensive part. The whole talk is that trade changing sign.
