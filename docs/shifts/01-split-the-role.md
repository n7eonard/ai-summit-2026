# Shift 1 — Split the role before it splits itself

**Slides:** 20–21 · **Evidence:** field + [Andrew Ng](../../evidence/andrew-ng-pm-ratio.md)

## The failure

You are doing both jobs. You believe you are doing both jobs. You are not — one of them has
been quietly eating the other for weeks, and the reason you can't see it is that the one
being eaten leaves no trace when it's skipped.

> Building is measurable, immediate, and nobody argues with it.
> That is exactly what makes it **a good place to hide.**

Deciding is slow, uncomfortable, produces no artefact at the end of the day, and every hour
spent on it looks from the outside like an hour of nothing. Building produces a commit.

Put both in one person under any pressure at all and there is no contest. The role doesn't
split evenly; it collapses into the half with the dopamine.

**From the field:** my highest-output week on that mission was also the week I pushed back on my
client the least. Those weren't independent events. The output *was* the avoidance.

## Why now specifically

The two jobs used to be self-balancing, because building was slow enough that you were
forced to think while you waited. Compile times, review queues, the sheer hours — the gaps
in the building were where the deciding happened.

Agents removed the gaps. There is no longer any dead time in which judgement happens for
free. It now has to be scheduled, or it doesn't happen.

## What to do

**Split it structurally, not by intention.** "I'll make sure to spend time on the why" is
not a plan; it's the thing everyone believes on the week they stop doing it.

- **Two people, if you have two people.** Ng's team asking for two PMs per engineer sounds
  absurd until you notice they're describing a ratio between *deciding capacity* and
  *building capacity*, and that building capacity just went up 8×.
- **If you're solo, split by time and by mode.** Different blocks, different tools,
  different outputs. The deciding block has to produce an artefact — a written position, a
  question sent to someone, a disagreement logged — or it will lose to the building block
  every time, because the building block always has something to show.
- **Make the deciding half legible to whoever you report to.** If your client or manager
  only sees shipped things, you have built an incentive system that punishes the half of
  your job that matters most.

## Objections

**"We're too small to split the role."** Then you're exactly the case this is about. Big
teams have the split by default; small teams have it collapse by default.

**"This is just 'do product properly'."** It is — but the reason it stops happening is new.
It used to fail from lack of skill or lack of headcount. It now fails from output being too
easy and too rewarding.
