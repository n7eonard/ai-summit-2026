## Shift candidates for the open fourth slot

Three real options, in order of how well they fit the talk's claim.

**1 — Rebuild what used to force the deciding to happen.** Not written yet; it depends on the
twelve-year half of the vantage (Q24). The review, the kickoff, the person whose job was to
disagree — these existed, they were load-bearing, and they disappeared along with the friction
that justified them. Nobody replaced them on purpose. This is the candidate that lands on the
talk's own claim rather than drifting into general product advice. → Q26

**2 — "You can't challenge what you can't read."** From April's second shift (*PMs: ship code,
or hire PMs who do*). Not about PMs becoming engineers — about the standing needed to disagree
with a build. Sharp, and it connects directly to the judge-and-party problem.

**3 — The chimera role.** An engineer asked to carry product responsibility ends up doing
neither job properly; reported consequences are friction and burnout. Aimed squarely at
engineers, who currently have no concrete moment in the talk (Q16). **Unsourced** — see below.

## Unverified market evidence

⛔ **Nothing here may go on a slide as it stands.** Gathered in a research conversation of
19 April 2026, never independently checked.

The value: it suggests the compression is visible in how AI-native companies hire, which would
turn a first-person claim into an observable shift — and directly reduce the "~60% first
person" problem (Q17).

- **Forward Deployed Engineer** — the role reported to be absorbing product work; embedded with
  a single customer to improve business processes with technology. a16z frames the pattern as
  *services-led growth*.
- **"T-shaped builders" over classical PMs** — AI-native companies reported hiring fewer PMs or
  delaying the first PM hire.
- **Adam Judelson**, attributed: the non-technical PM is dead — the middle layers focused on
  stakeholder management and process orchestration are what gets automated.
- **Gergely Orosz**, attributed: product-mindedness is becoming a baseline expectation at
  startups, not a specialisation.
- **The counter-evidence, which matters most.** Mistral reported actively hiring PMs, explicitly
  open to engineering backgrounds moving into product. Anthropic recruiting PMs at high
  compensation for a very different profile. So the honest reading is not "the PM is
  disappearing" — it's that coordination and translation layers collapse while sharp product
  thinking survives in whoever builds.

**If any of this is used, source it first.** In priority order: the chimera role and its burnout
claim (most quotable, therefore most dangerous unsourced), then Judelson, Orosz, the a16z piece,
then live dated job postings.
